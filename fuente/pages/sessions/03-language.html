'Pataphysics

Alfred Jarry: the science of imaginary solutions, the science of the particular. It studies the laws of exceptions, not of rules.

Its key word is clinamen, from Epicurus and Lucretius. Atoms falling straight and parallel through the void would never touch; nothing would ever form. So now and then one swerves, a tiny bit, at no fixed time or place. It hits a neighbour, and the collisions make the world. The clinamen is that swerve: a small deviation nobody planned, which makes something happen.

The Collège de 'Pataphysique, Paris, 1948. Duchamp, Miró, Ionesco, Queneau, Vian passed through it.

Fernando Arrabal (Melilla, 1932): Transcendent Satrap of the Collège. In 1962, with Alejandro Jodorowsky and Roland Topor, the Panic Movement, after the god Pan. Its springs: memory, chance, confusion. For him 'pataphysics is not empty or hermetic: its works carry a meaning as confused as existence itself.

Sources: Pataphysics · Fernando Arrabal · Panic Movement · Arrabal, du transcendant corps des satrapes

The text

Fernando Arrabal, Mi anatomía explicada (My Anatomy Explained). A painting of himself, explained: fourteen parts of a body, each one a symbol. Not a story: a map.

Language is not a photo

A prompt is not a photograph of what you want. The gap between the words and the picture is where the machine invents. We like that.

Different languages

Same thing, same seed, four languages: different results. Slightly, but different.

"A black bowler hat, alone; its crown swells and contracts like a beating heart."

Language used wrong

Broken grammar. Words from a zone: stoop, morrocoy. Same seed, no style words.

A picture made into language

Take a picture you feel connected to and have an LLM put it into words. Given the picture itself, the model copies it or ignores it. Given its description, it takes it somewhere.

A little about time

Time words in a prompt barely move the model. Time as an operation on the pixels does.

From frame to map

A picture is a frame. Break the frame: many frames, movable. The pictures and the little videos become a space you build: a map, an anatomy, not a story.

Patamapa

A page you drop videos and pictures on. They are thrown onto the canvas; you place them. The map is yours: where a thing sits, how big it is, what touches what.

download patamapa.html   try it here

  1. Put your videos and pictures in one folder.
  2. Open patamapa.html in Chrome or Edge.
  3. Drag the folder onto the page.
  4. Drag to move. Pull the corner to resize. Double-click a name to change it. ♪ turns a video's sound on, × removes it.
  5. save writes patamapa.html into your folder, next to the videos. Open that one: the map is there, and you can keep working on it.

One file, nothing to install, runs without internet. The saved map finds its videos by name, so they must stay in the same folder.

It is small on purpose. Things it could learn: an infinite canvas (left, right, up, down, forever), text on the map, rotation, masks and mats that give a video a shape, veins that connect two parts and pulse. It is one HTML file: open it, read it, ask a chat to change it.

Patamapa v2: words

The same map, now with words. The words are laid out with Pretext (by Cheng Lou, MIT), bundled inside the file.

download patamapa v2   try it here

  • Words come in with + text, by pasting (Ctrl+V), or by dropping a .txt file (with the folder too).
  • Whatever is on top wins: a picture laid over words pushes them aside; words laid over a picture sit on it.
  • Hover the words: A− A+ size, ◯ pour them into a circle (they grow to fill it), ¶ back to a paragraph, ⁘ scatter the paragraph into loose words.
  • Double-click to write. save keeps the words inside the map.

The prompts, taken apart

One part of the map, the heart, from the words to the video. Two steps: words → a still (Qwen 2.1, workflow 04), then the still → a video (LTX 2.5, workflow 02). The still is the video's first frame.

The picture prompt

from the textUn sombrero hongo negro, solo; su copa se hincha y se contrae como un corazón que late. (A black bowler hat, alone; its crown swells and contracts like a beating heart.)

from a picture I feel connected to, described by an LLMLong parallel contour lines wrapping every form like wood grain or roots, moss green showing through, soft forest light; small orange accents. Pen-and-ink drawing over a smeared, blurred photograph, from Victor's animated film Bosquejo Raiamoto.

The video prompt

the action: how this part of the map behaves, and what it sounds likeThe black hat swells and contracts like a beating heart, in steady regular beats. Sound: a deep heartbeat, lub-dub, lub-dub.

good practice: the picture's description again, shortThe camera holds completely still. A pen-and-ink drawing over a smeared photograph: the ink lines shimmer and crawl, the dots and hatching boil gently, as in a hand-drawn animation.

In the lab

  1. Take your text. Choose at least 5 parts of it: things, places, organs, not events.
  2. For each part, write what it is in one sentence.
  3. Find a picture you feel connected to. Ask an LLM to describe it in words.
  4. Picture prompt = your sentence + that description. Qwen 2.1 text to image (workflow 04). You get a still.
  5. Video prompt = how it lives and what it sounds like (+ the description again, short). LTX 2.5 image to video (workflow 02), your still as the first frame. You get a video.
  6. Put the videos and stills in one folder, drop it on Patamapa, place them, save.
