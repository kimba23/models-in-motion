My Anatomy Explained

Fernando Arrabal · Mi anatomía explicada

My Biography — d) Anatomy

When she came to the house, I showed her the painting My Anatomy Explained and talked her through it:

“In the centre of the painting is my full-length portrait. The two columns, one on either side, are divided into seven images each, representing the fourteen symbols of my anatomy.

“My head is symbolized by a battleship in a cage, my brain by a beehive in which the head of a man in a top hat appears, my eyes by a sun and a travelling trunk, and my ears by half a head swimming, bearing the words ‘Mens, intellectus, ratio’.

“At my feet stands a dragon with seven wolves’ heads and a sceptre. This dragon has wings. There is an opening in my body running from my neck to my crotch, passing through my hips, like an enormous wound that reveals my heart, my intestines and other organs.

“My chest is represented by the Colossus of Thebes, inscribed with my name, my sex by the battlemented terrace of a castle above which floats a large egg, my liver by a telephone with a foetus in the receiver, my intestines by a bull’s head and a chalice containing three vipers, and, surprising as it may seem, my heart is symbolized by a bowler hat above it.

“I am in a medieval room with an arcade of columns. In the distance, a landscape with Gothic churches can be glimpsed. The floor is laid with tiles decorated in geometric patterns.

“My feet are represented by a centaur and an animal that is half cat, half fish, my hands by the earth beside the man hanging by his feet, my legs by a chess knight in the middle of a field, my arms by two fingers pierced by an arrow, and my navel by a pair of scales with eyes and clocks in the pans.

“My face sports a fringe of beard, and I feign indifference to the fourteen symbols. My expression is stern, and my right hand is close to my mouth, almost as though I were about to raise a pipe to it.”

When I had finished my explanation, she asked me:

“Don’t you see that the painting you’re telling me about is only a mirror?”
